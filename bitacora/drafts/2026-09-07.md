## Post

Every AI-generated action needs exactly one moment where a human can say no. Put it in the wrong place and it's worthless.

This week I was deciding where that moment belongs in a flow where a system proposes something and a person has to approve it before it goes live.

Too early, and you're asking someone to judge a plan with no context — they rubber-stamp it because they can't yet tell good from bad.

Too late, and the thing is already halfway out the door; "reviewing" becomes a formality, not a real check.

I moved the checkpoint to the one spot where the person has just enough information to actually evaluate it, and not so much that reviewing feels like redoing the work themselves.

Good judgment is a scarcer resource than people admit. It isn't available all day, on demand, whenever you decide to ask for it — it depletes. Which is exactly why the hardest calls in any system belong earlier, not later: you're spending someone's dwindling judgment budget, and where you place that ask matters as much as the ask itself.

Same rule applies to yourself. Your best judgment lives in a narrow window each day. Spend it on the one decision that actually needs it — not on the twentieth small thing that drained you first.

Where in your day — or your product — is the "human checkpoint" sitting in the wrong place?
